# Candidates — Museum of the Year 3000

Bounty: `u-donrulezz-museum-of-the-year-3000-b727a0bb` — one line, the museum label
a museum in the year 3000 puts beside an ordinary object from today.
Gate: UTF-8, 1–200 characters, one line, English, original.

Judge criteria, in order: (1) on-subject museum label for an ordinary object
from today; (2) joke lands on first read, laugh inside the line; (3) humour
from the year 3000 misreading the object — true AND absurd about what it was
for; (4) reads like a real museum label — short, deadpan, confident, one line;
(5) object and angle fresh, not the obvious first joke about the obvious
first object.

Deliberately avoided: the RULES examples (plastic fork, parking meter, house
key, group chat) — those are the objects every other agent will pick first.

## Ranking (against the judge's five criteria, in order)

1. **C1 — CAPTCHA, citizens.** Strongest on every criterion. The misreading
   is the museum's own: a CAPTCHA as a civic humanity test (true — that is
   literally what it was for) made absurd by traffic lights and by "most
   failed twice", a concrete surprising detail. Deadpan, confident, one line.
   Fresh object, fresh angle — nobody's first joke about a fork.
2. **C2 — Terms checkbox.** Nearly as strong: the checkbox as a "sacred oath"
   is a perfect year-3000 misreading, and "no human who swore it had ever
   read them" lands instantly. Loses to C1 only because its punchline is a
   universal claim rather than a specific surprising detail.
3. **C3 — Password.** Same true-and-absurd shape as C2 (guarded fiercely,
   then written on paper), but the twist is slightly more expected.
4. **C4 — Typing indicator.** Fresh object, true ("often never arrived"),
   but "historians note" softens the deadpan and the laugh is gentler.
5. **C5 — Elevator button.** Beautifully deadpan ("It changed nothing. It
   was pressed anyway.") but the joke is about a useless button, not about
   the year 3000 misreading the object — weaker on criterion 3.
6. **C8 — CAPTCHA, Mara Voss.** Same joke as C1 with a named person, but
   the institutional "citizens" voice reads more like a real museum label
   (criterion 4) than a single anecdote.
7. **C7 — Voicemail.** True and funny, but "call me back" is a familiar
   joke shape; the misreading is thin.
8. **C6 — Loading wheel.** "Certain that waiting was progress" is clever
   but drifts toward commentary rather than a label, and it is a touch long.

## The pick

**C1** — the entry in `main.py` is exactly this line:

> CAPTCHA, c. 2024. To prove they were human, citizens identified traffic lights — a test most failed twice.

106 characters, one line, UTF-8, English, original.
